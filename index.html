The story we are watching

Improvement over time matters.

Single-year percentages can move quickly, particularly where cohorts are relatively small. We therefore look carefully at trends, national context, pupil groups and the wider evidence behind each result — not simply whether one comparator is higher or lower.

06 · Understanding the data

What do these measures mean?

Statutory assessment language can be technical. These short explanations are designed to help families read the figures with confidence.

Expected standard

The level pupils are expected to reach by the end of the relevant statutory assessment point.

Higher standard / greater depth

A higher level of attainment. At KS2, reading and mathematics use scaled test scores; writing uses teacher assessment.

Scaled score

At KS2, a scaled score of 100 represents the expected standard. A score of 110 or more represents the higher standard in reading and mathematics.

Why percentages can move

Each cohort is different. Small changes in pupil numbers can create noticeable percentage shifts, so we always consider trends and context as well as a single year's result.
